# Heated particle drop

Open **ParticleBeakerDrop**. In Edit Mode select **Three Phase 3D Apparatus → Particle Beaker Drop** and enable **Heat Moving Water** under **Thermal Coupling**. The default release is 9 mL. Enable **Larger Beaker** under **Release Settings** for 250 mL. In Play Mode set **Burner** to 0 W and click **Measure drop + slosh**, then repeat with 10 W. Each measurement resets positions, particle temperature and the glass field. The console reports particle and timing diagnostics alongside absorbed burner energy, energy stored in solids and water, and the balance residual. For longer heating use **Run**. Water markers are blue at 20 °C and red at 30 °C by default. The Game panel displays the temperature scale. Lower **Red endpoint** toward 20.1 °C to make early warming visible. This changes the colour mapping only.

The GPU carries each temperature through the particle sort, conducts heat through the glass and support, and transfers heat between the base and particles within one nominal spacing of the floor. Each radial annulus caps the effective wetted area at its geometric area. The glass loses the same signed energy that the particles gain. The thermal step is split from the mechanical batch and substeps at a conservative explicit limit. Heat stays on the GPU during a run. The benchmark reads back the inventories to check the balance.

This is a floor-contact integration with the existing particle drop. Glass and liquid properties are constant for the check. The test is insulated and the thermal field does not yet change particle buoyancy or exchange heat at the wall. The 0.4 s benchmark is intended for regression and energy bookkeeping. Heating over longer simulated times is needed to see a colour change at 10 W.
